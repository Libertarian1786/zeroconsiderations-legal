TargetScan Alternative: What Happens After the Group Is Measured

TargetScan is one of the originals of photo shot-group measurement on iPhone — it's been on the App Store for over a decade, measures groups from a photo, keeps a shooting diary, and holds a small but very happy rating base (around 4.8 stars across a few hundred ratings, at $17.99, as of this writing). Respect where due: it proved this category before phones were good at it.

Who's writing this: the developer of Zero Considerations — one of the apps below. Weigh accordingly. Competitor facts are taken from their public store listings and sites as of September 2026, and where an app is the better pick for a job, the page says so.

Three practical differences

1. Platforms

TargetScan lives on the App Store; as of this writing we could find no Android listing. Zero Considerations is on both stores at $9.99 — relevant if your range buddies split between platforms, or you ever switch.

2. Price

$17.99 vs $9.99, both one-time. Neither runs a subscription — a thing worth honoring in both directions in this category.

3. What happens after the measurement

This is the real fork. A measured group is an answer to "how did I shoot?" Zero Considerations treats it as the input to "what do I dial?":

  • Exact turret clicks for your optic's click value and direction, from the measured offset — dial, then confirm with a group.
  • Truing — muzzle velocity and BC backed out from photographed drop at a known distance.
  • Full solver and DOPE — drop, wind, energy in MOA, MIL, or clicks.
  • 215 to-scale reticles — see your holdovers on your actual reticle.
  • Scale from coded markers — the free printable targets carry coded corner markers that set the photo's true scale and correct camera tilt/perspective before anything is measured.
