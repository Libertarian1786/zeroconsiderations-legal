Strelok Pro alternatives (2026): the honest guide

Strelok and Strelok Pro have been gone from Google Play and the Apple App Store since 2023, and the million-plus shooters who relied on them are still asking the same question in every long-range forum: what do I use now?

This page is our attempt at a straight answer. We make one of the apps below, so read with that in mind — but we'll tell you plainly when a competitor is the better pick for your use case, because a shooter who buys the wrong app doesn't come back.

What actually happened to Strelok

In early 2023, Strelok, Strelok Pro, and Strelok+ disappeared from both major app stores and never came back.

The reason was not gun content. This gets repeated wrong in comment sections constantly, so it's worth being precise: ballistic calculators are still all over both stores, and nothing about the category was restricted. Strelok's developer, Igor Borisov, is Russian. After sanctions were imposed on Russia following the invasion of Ukraine, Apple and Google could no longer legally process payments to Russian developers. Pulling the apps was a payments-and-compliance decision forced by sanctions law — not a policy statement about shooting apps.

That distinction matters practically, not just as trivia. If the category had been banned, every app on this page would be at risk. It wasn't, and they aren't.

Where that leaves you depends on your phone:

  • iPhone: a dead end. New users can't buy Strelok Pro at all, and it can't be re-downloaded from the App Store. If you still have it installed, it keeps working — until a phone replacement or a migration hiccup strands you, with no updates coming.
  • Android: a side door exists. Strelok Pro is still listed on the Samsung Galaxy Store and Huawei AppGallery. If you run a Samsung phone and are comfortable buying through the Galaxy Store, you can still get the genuine article. But you're outside the mainstream update path, a Google Play purchase from years past can't be restored there, and the long-term maintenance picture is anyone's guess.
Strelok® is the work of its own developer and this page isn't affiliated with it. Credit where due: for over a decade it was the best value in ballistic software, and much of what's below is measured against the standard it set.

Still have Strelok installed? Rescue your data first

Whichever replacement you pick, do this before your old install dies, because the data inside it is worth more than the app at this point. Years of trued velocities don't come back on their own. Ten minutes now saves a season of re-zeroing later:

  • Screenshot every rifle profile. Every screen of it — cartridge, bullet, scope, atmosphere defaults. Screenshots survive a dead app; an internal database doesn't.
  • Write down the trued muzzle velocity for each load. This is the single most valuable number you own. If you ever calibrated Strelok against real drops, the MV it settled on is the product of live fire, not a box-flap guess.
  • Record the BC (and whether it's G1 or G7) plus bullet weight for each load. Mixing up the drag model when re-entering data is the classic migration error, and it quietly wrecks solutions past 300 yards.
  • Note each scope's click value, reticle, and focal plane, and your sight height and twist rate. Sight height especially — people measure it once, forget it, and then guess wrong in the new app.
  • Capture your confirmed zeroes — distance, load, and the conditions you zeroed in.

Every app in the table below will take those numbers. None of them can recover the data for you once the old install is gone.

What Strelok users say they miss most

Read any "Strelok replacement" thread — Sniper's Hide, AccurateShooter, Rokslide, the Reddit long-range subs — and the same answer comes up first, almost every time: the reticle database.

Strelok Pro drew thousands of reticles to scale. You picked your exact scope, set your magnification, and saw your drop and wind hold overlaid on the actual hash marks of the actual reticle in front of your eye. No other mainstream app matched it then, and most still don't try.

Why it matters at the range: a numeric solver tells you "come up 1.7 mil, hold 0.4 left." That's useful if you're dialing. But if you're holding over — hunting, positional stages, anything where there's no time to spin a turret — you need to know what 1.7 mil looks like in your reticle, at your magnification. On a second-focal-plane scope the subtensions change with power, and doing that conversion in your head at the moment of truth is how misses happen.

For BDC reticle owners it's even more important. The yardage numbers etched into a BDC reticle are only true for one load, at one velocity, in one atmosphere. A proper reticle view re-maps those marks to what your load actually does — which is the difference between a BDC being a tool and being a decoration.

After the reticle view, the misses people name are simpler: Strelok was cheap, one-time purchase, and fully offline. Keep those three in mind as you read the table below — the industry has drifted toward subscriptions since 2023, and not everyone has followed.

The honest mini-reviews

Strelok Pro — if you can still get it

Still an excellent solver attached to the deepest reticle catalog ever shipped. If you're on a Samsung phone, buying it through the Galaxy Store is a legitimate option and we won't pretend otherwise. The trade-offs are real, though: you're on an alternative store, updates are no longer flowing through the channels most people use, and you're investing in software whose future has been uncertain since 2023. For iPhone owners it simply isn't a choice anymore.

Applied Ballistics — AB Quantum

The most respected solver in the business, full stop. Bryan Litz's custom drag curves are the reference standard, the same engine runs inside Kestrel wind meters, and serious ELR and PRS competitors trust it for a reason. The current app, AB Quantum, replaced the old one-time-purchase AB Mobile app in late 2024 with a tier model: a free Ultralite level, an Elite subscription at $2.99 a month or $19.99 a year, and free tier unlocks if you own AB-powered hardware like a Kestrel. The honest knocks: it's complex — the depth that competitors love is overhead for a hunter zeroing one rifle — and the subscription era grates on shooters who bought Strelok once and used it for a decade. No Strelok-style reticle drawing; solutions are numeric. If you shoot matches at distance, this is the serious tool and you should probably have it. It just isn't trying to be Strelok.

Hornady 4DOF

The best free ballistic app, and it isn't close. Hornady's 4DOF calculator models trajectory from Doppler-radar-derived drag coefficients rather than a ballistic coefficient, accounts for effects like aerodynamic jump, and costs nothing. It runs offline and includes measured data for a wide library of long-range projectiles — Hornady's own plus popular bullets from Sierra, Berger, and others. The limits: the deepest modeling applies to bullets in its library, there's no reticle overlay to speak of, and there are no zeroing or photo tools — it tells you the correction, and the rest is on you. If your bullet is in the library and all you want is a firing solution, get it — it's free on both app stores.

Shooter

One of the original serious mobile ballistic apps, around since 2010, with a solver built on Bryan Litz's work. Clean interface, Kestrel and WeatherFlow Bluetooth support, and a straightforward $19.99 one-time purchase — no subscription, which earns real respect in 2026. It's a solver in the classic mold: numbers in, firing solution out. No reticle catalog, no photo tools. A solid, proven choice for shooters who want exactly that and nothing else (shooterapp.net).

GeoBallistics — BallisticsARC

The mapping app. GeoBallistics' distinctive strength is building range cards on satellite imagery: plot your firing position and targets on the map, and it computes ranges and solutions for the whole stage. Field-match and NRL Hunter shooters swear by it. The base app is free with a one-time $14.99 Pro unlock — no subscription, which fits the Strelok-refugee sensibility. It's not a reticle app and it's not a zeroing app; it's the strongest pick when the problem is "unknown distances across a canyon," not "get this scope dialed."

Ballistic-X

The focused group-measurement tool, at $7.99. Photograph your target, tap each bullet hole, and it computes group size, distance from point of aim, and a scope correction. Version 3.0.3 (June 2025) added its own BX targets with a printed auto-reference that sets photo scale automatically, which removed its old manual-scale step. It does its one job well and cheap. The differences from our approach, stated plainly: in Ballistic-X you mark each hole by hand (per its own store description), while Zero Considerations auto-detects holes; ZC's four coded corner markers also correct camera tilt and perspective, not just scale; and Ballistic-X hands you a measurement and correction, while ZC carries the same photo on into turret clicks, MV/BC truing, DOPE, and reticle holdovers. After a quiet stretch following the June 2025 release, it shipped a complete v4 rewrite in spring 2026 and has kept updating since.

Zero Considerations — where we fit

The reticle side: 215 reticle patterns, drawn from the manufacturer's own published subtension figures wherever the maker publishes them — not traced off marketing images. Pick your scope, set your magnification, and see your holdovers on the reticle you'll actually be looking through. Where a manufacturer publishes no numbers under that name, the reticle is drawn from the closest documented pattern instead of invented subtensions.

The zeroing side: print a free target with coded corner markers (or lay a cartridge on any target for scale), shoot your group, photograph it. Frequently asked questions

Is Strelok Pro still available in 2026?

Not on Google Play or the Apple App Store — it was removed from both in 2023 and hasn't returned. On Android it's still listed on the Samsung Galaxy Store and Huawei AppGallery. On iPhone there's no way for a new user to get it.

Was Strelok banned for gun content?

No. Ballistic calculators remain freely available on both stores. The removal happened because sanctions on Russia made it illegal for Apple and Google to process payments to the app's Russian developer. It was a compliance matter, not a content decision.

What replaces Strelok's reticle view?

That's the hard one — most big-name solvers (AB Quantum, 4DOF, Shooter, GeoBallistics) give numeric solutions and don't draw your reticle. Zero Considerations draws 215 reticle patterns from manufacturers' published subtension figures with holdovers overlaid at your magnification, which is the closest current equivalent to what Strelok did.

What's the best free Strelok alternative?

Hornady 4DOF — free, offline, and built on Doppler-derived drag data that's genuinely excellent for the bullets in its library. AB Quantum's Ultralite tier is also free. Neither shows your reticle.

Does Strelok Pro still work if I already have it?

An installed copy generally keeps calculating. But it can't be updated through the mainstream stores, and on iPhone it can't be re-downloaded — one phone replacement can end it for good. Worth planning your exit before that day rather than after.

Which apps measure shot groups from a photo?

The two best-known in the zeroing space: Ballistic-X ($7.99 — you tap each hole yourself) and Zero Considerations ($9.99 — holes auto-detected, coded markers correct tilt and scale, and the same photo flows into turret clicks, truing, and DOPE). Target-scoring apps like TargetScan also read shots from a photo for scoring.
