Sight-In Targets for 200 Yards — the Open-Country Zero

A 200-yard zero is the flat-country hunter's answer to "how do I not think about holdover." With many modern hunting loads, zeroed at 200 the bullet stays within a few inches of your line of sight from the muzzle out past 250 — which covers the vast majority of ethical hunting shots with a plain center hold. Here's how to set one up, including from a 100-yard range.

  • 1-inch grid — for the 100-yard offset method below, where exact inches matter.
  • Silhouette — confirm on a full-size shape at distance.

Two ways to get there

The direct way: shoot it at 200

If your range has a 200-yard line, zero there directly. Groups open up and wind starts to vote, so shoot from a solid rest and let a measured group — not a called flyer — drive the correction. Photograph the target up close after the string; the measurement workflow is the same as at any distance.

The common way: offset at 100

Most people set a 200-yard zero from the 100-yard line by printing deliberately high. The much-quoted figure is around 1.5 to 2 inches high at 100 for classic flat-shooting hunting loads — real for many setups, wrong for plenty of others. Slower cartridges need more, and sight height moves the number too. The honest process: compute the 100-yard offset for your load and sight height (the app's solver does this), shoot to that offset on the 1-inch grid, and confirm at actual distance the first chance you get.

Know your maximum point-blank range. A 200-yard zero's real payoff is the span over which a center hold stays in the vitals. The app's point-blank-range tool computes that span for your load and a vital-zone size you choose — worth checking before you decide 200 is even the right zero for your cartridge.

The three-shot sight-in workflow

  1. Print the target at 100% scale ("actual size" — never "fit to page"). Every sheet has a 1-inch calibration bar so you can check your printer with a ruler.
  2. Shoot a 3-round group from a solid rest, aiming center.
  3. Photograph the target with the Zero Considerations app. The coded corner markers set true scale and correct camera tilt, the bullet holes are found automatically, and the app converts your group's offset into the exact click count for your optic's click value.
  4. Dial the correction, then shoot a confirmation group. A group is a sample — the confirmation group is what makes it a zero.
