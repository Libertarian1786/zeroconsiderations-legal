BallisticsARC: When You Want Maps — and When You Want a Zero

BallisticsARC by GeoBallistics (Vortex-owned since 2023) has one differentiator no other app in this category matches: range cards built from GPS mapping. Mark your firing point, mark your steel, and get ranges and solutions tied to actual terrain. For open-country steel shooters and hunters who plan positions on a map, that's a real tool, and if that's your shooting, it earns its install.

Who's writing this: the developer of Zero Considerations — one of the apps below. Weigh accordingly. Competitor facts are taken from their public store listings and sites as of September 2026, and where an app is the better pick for a job, the page says so.

Different jobs entirely

BallisticsARC works on the terrain: where things are and what the solution to reach them is. Zero Considerations works on the target: where your group actually landed and what to do about it.

  • Photograph the shot target once — target face and bullet holes found automatically, old holes and the backer excluded, group measured in real inches.
  • The offset becomes an exact click count for your optic's click value and direction — dial, then confirm with a group.
  • True your muzzle velocity from photographed drop at a known distance.
  • 218 reticles drawn to published subtensions; height-over-bore from your optic and mount; whole-rig loadouts with separate zeros for primary, offset, laser, and irons.

The honest recommendation

If your shooting is planned on maps — mapped steel, mapped stands, open country — keep BallisticsARC for that; nothing here does its mapping job. If your pain is the bench — sight-in day, a new optic, a load change, holdovers you can actually see — that loop is Zero Considerations' whole reason to exist, and the two apps don't overlap enough to conflict.

For education and lawful sporting use. Figures here are rough and load-dependent — treat them as orientation, not dope. Always confirm your zero with live fire at your real distance before relying on it, and follow all firearm safety rules and your range's procedures.
