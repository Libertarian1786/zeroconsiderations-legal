Strelok Pro APK download — read this first

Why Strelok Pro disappeared from the app stores

Strelok and Strelok Pro were built and maintained for over a decade by Igor Borisov, a Russian developer, and racked up millions of downloads. In February 2023 the U.S. State Department added Borisov to the sanctions list under Executive Order 14024, stating that the apps had reportedly been used by Russian forces in Ukraine.

Once a developer is on that list, U.S. companies can no longer process his payments or distribute his software. Apple and Google pulled Strelok and Strelok Pro within weeks — and because the removal happened server-side, even people who paid for the app can't re-download it from their purchase history. New phone, factory reset, accidental delete: gone.

What this was not: a policy decision about shooting apps. Ballistic calculators are alive and well on both stores today. This was a sanctions-compliance action against one specific developer's ability to receive money and distribute software through U.S. companies.

That's the whole story of why the APK is "floating around." A popular paid app was orphaned overnight with an enormous user base and no official download link. Mirror sites moved into that vacuum immediately, and they've owned the search results ever since.

What the APK sites are actually serving

Look closely at what those search results offer: archives of version 6.8.x tagged "paid," "premium," "pro unlocked," "free download." Walk through what each of those labels means for an app that cost real money on Google Play:

  • "Unlocked" means cracked. Strelok Pro was a paid app. A free copy that runs without a license check is a copy someone has already modified. A binary that's been opened up and re-signed once can carry anything else the re-packer felt like adding — and repackaged builds of popular orphaned apps are a well-worn malware delivery route on Android.
  • Even "honest" mirrors can't solve the trust problem. Some sites do host what they claim is an untouched archived APK. But you have no chain of custody. Normally you'd verify an APK's signing certificate against a known-good copy — here the "known-good" copies come from the same class of websites. There is nothing official left to check against.
  • No updates, ever. Whatever you sideload is the last version you will ever have. No bug fixes, no new bullet data, no compatibility patches.
  • No support. There's nobody to email when the app misbehaves, and the mirror site certainly isn't going to troubleshoot your dope.
  • The developer gets nothing. Whatever you think of the sanctions, a cracked APK doesn't pay the man who wrote the app — it pays the mirror site's ad network.

Even a clean APK is on a countdown

Suppose you beat the odds and get a genuine, unmodified Strelok Pro build. It's still frozen in early 2023. Every major Android release changes something — permission models, camera behavior, background limits, storage access — and an app that can never be updated never gets patched for any of it. Android has also been steadily raising the floor on how old an app can be and still install cleanly. When a frozen app breaks on a future phone, it stays broken. That's not a knock on the app; it's what happens to any software once maintenance becomes impossible.

The legitimate paths that still exist

Your own old phone

If you bought Strelok Pro and still have a phone with it installed, that installed copy is the one APK on earth whose history you actually know. You can extract your own app and move it to your new device (search "adb extract apk" for the procedure). You still inherit the no-updates problem, but not the tampering problem.

Huawei AppGallery

Strelok Pro remained available on Huawei's AppGallery well after the Play Store removal — download guides from 2024 and 2025 describe getting it there, with updates, via a Huawei account. Your old Google Play purchase does not transfer; it's a new purchase on their storefront.

Samsung Galaxy Store

The Galaxy Store carried it for a stretch as well, but recent user reports are mixed, with some regions unable to download it. Availability on both alternative stores varies by country and has shifted over time — check on your own device rather than trusting a forum post from 2023.

One wrinkle worth knowing before you re-buy anywhere: the sanctions exist precisely to stop new payments flowing to the designated developer, and they bind U.S. persons and companies. Whether a purchase through a non-U.S. storefront is permissible where you live is a question for you and your jurisdiction — we're not offering legal advice, just making sure you go in with eyes open.

Frequently asked questions

Is it illegal to keep using Strelok Pro?

The sanctions restrict U.S. companies from processing the developer's payments and distributing his software — that's why the stores pulled it. They don't reach into your phone and criminalize an app you already bought; plenty of shooters still run their old installs. New purchases are the murkier area, especially for U.S. buyers. This isn't legal advice.

Is it safe to download the Strelok Pro APK from a mirror site?

It can't be verified, which is the problem. Free copies of a paid app are cracked by definition, and even sites hosting supposedly untouched archives give you no official signature to check against. You'd be trusting an anonymous re-uploader with an app you'll use at a firing range. We'd pass.

Can I still buy Strelok Pro anywhere?

As of this writing, Huawei's AppGallery has been the most consistently reported legitimate source, and the Samsung Galaxy Store has carried it in some regions at some times. Availability varies by country, previous Google Play purchases don't transfer, and U.S. buyers should read the sanctions note above before sending new money.

Will Strelok Pro come back to Google Play or the App Store?

Only if the sanctions designation is lifted or specifically licensed, and as of this writing (August 2026) there's no public sign of either. Until that changes, Apple and Google legally cannot carry it.
