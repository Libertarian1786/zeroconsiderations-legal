Stop guessing at the bench

Shoot a group and photograph the target once. Mark your aim point, type the target's size, and the app finds the target and every bullet hole on its own — measures the group in real inches and tells you how many clicks to turn, for your optic, your load, and your distance. About half a minute, all on the phone. Then it can work backwards from that group to true your muzzle velocity and BC, so your dope matches the rifle you actually own.

Photo zeroing

One photo after shooting. The app finds the target face and every hole itself, rings your rounds — skipping old holes and the backer — and converts point-of-impact error into exact turret clicks for your optic's click value. Dial it, then confirm with a group.

Zero and true

Back out true muzzle velocity and ballistic coefficient from a photographed group at a known distance.

To-scale reticles

See your actual reticle at magnification with real subtensions, so you know your holdovers before you shoot.

Full solver

Drop, wind, and energy in MOA, MIL, or clicks, with environment pulled from your phone's sensors.

Whole rig, not one optic

Primary optic, 45° offset, laser, and irons — each with its own height over bore and its own zero.

Genuinely offline

Every measurement and every ballistic solution runs on the phone. No account, no signup, no ads, no tracking, no signal required. The one network call in the app is the optional weather auto-fill you tap.

Built on published numbers, not guesses

Every preset traces back to a manufacturer's published figure. Where a manufacturer doesn't publish something, the app says so instead of inventing it — and where no maker publishes a subtension table under a given reticle name, that reticle is drawn from the closest documented pattern rather than from made-up numbers.

Questions

Does it need cell service?

No. Everything runs on the phone. The one optional network call is pulling current weather, and only if you tap the button.

Do I need an account?

No account, no email, no login. Your rifles, loads, and photos never leave your device.

Will it replace a chronograph?

No. It trues your velocity from observed drop, which is a good practical estimate, not a chronograph reading.

Is it a rangefinder?

It can estimate range from a photo of a known-size object. That's useful at short and mid range, not a substitute for a laser rangefinder.
