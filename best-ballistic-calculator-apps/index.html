The Best Ballistic Calculator Apps in 2026, by the Job You Need Done

"Best ballistic app" is the wrong question — the category splits into solvers, zeroing tools, mapping apps, and group-measurement apps, and the best pick depends on which job you're hiring for. Here's the honest field guide, including the free corporate apps that are genuinely excellent at what they do.

Who's writing this: the developer of Zero Considerations — one of the apps below. Weigh accordingly. Competitor facts are taken from their public store listings and sites as of September 2026, and where an app is the better pick for a job, the page says so.

Best free solver: Hornady 4DOF

Free · iOS & Android. A serious drag-model solver from a major ammunition maker, free with nothing held back behind an unlock — and with tens of thousands of store ratings averaging around 4.8 as of this writing. If all you want is a trustworthy trajectory table and your bullet is in its library, install it and pay nobody. The trade-offs: it exists to sell Hornady ammunition, and zeroing itself — measuring where your group landed and turning that into turret clicks — isn't its focus. Full comparison.

Deepest solver ecosystem: Applied Ballistics Quantum

Free tier + Elite subscription · iOS & Android. Applied Ballistics is the reference name in long-range solving, and Quantum (launched December 2024) is its current app: excellent free tier, custom drag models and advanced features in a paid Elite tier, unlocked free when paired with AB-equipped devices like Kestrel meters. For competitive long-range shooters already in that hardware world, this is the answer. The trade-off is the model: the advanced tier is a subscription, and the app's center of gravity is solving, not sight-in day. Full comparison.

Best for sight-in day and photo zeroing: Zero Considerations (ours)

$9.99 one-time · iOS & Android. Our app, so apply the disclosure above. The pitch in one line: photograph your shot target once and the app finds the target and every bullet hole on its own, measures the group in real inches, and gives you the exact click correction for your optic — then trues your velocity from the same photos, with 218 reticles drawn to published subtensions, 793 firearm and 512 load presets, offline, no account, no subscription. It's the only app in this list built around the zeroing loop itself rather than the trajectory table.

Group-measurement specialist: Ballistic-X

$7.99 one-time + optional yearly-subscription add-on · iOS & Android. The app that made photo group measurement mainstream: photograph the target, tap each hole, get group size and offset. Fast and focused. As of this writing its App Store rating sits at 3.7 with recent reviews citing v4 bugs — worth a look at current reviews before buying. If you only score groups and dope your own way, it does that one job at a fair price. Full comparison.

Best with mapping: BallisticsARC by GeoBallistics

Free + $14.99 one-time Pro · iOS & Android. Vortex-owned since 2023, and its differentiator is real: range cards built from GPS and mapping — mark your firing point and targets on a map and get ranges and solutions tied to actual terrain. If your shooting is steel in open country at mapped distances, it earns its slot. Full comparison.

The long-standing one-time solver: Shooter

$19.99 one-time · listed since 2010. One of the original paid mobile solvers, still on the stores with a small but very satisfied rating base. It shows its age in interface and pace of updates, but the solver mathematics haven't expired. A defensible pick for someone who wants a classic paid solver and nothing modern.
