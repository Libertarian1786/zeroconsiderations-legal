Sending a bug report costs you nothing and it is the only way I find out something is broken. The app collects no analytics and phones nothing home, so if you do not tell me, I do not know.

Reporting a bug

Most reports that stall do so for one reason: I cannot reproduce them. Five lines of context usually fixes that. Copy this block into your email and fill it in — anything you cannot answer, leave blank.

App version and build:   (Settings → About, e.g. "0.14.10 (build 100)")
Phone and OS:            (e.g. "Pixel 7, Android 15" or "iPhone 13, iOS 18.5")
Where it happened:       (which screen / which step of the session)
What you expected:
What actually happened:
Detection details text:  (paste it — see below)

1. The version and build number

Open the app → Settings → scroll to About. The line reads Version 0.14.10 (build 100) or similar. The build number matters more than the version — several fixes have shipped inside the same version string, and it tells me immediately whether you already have the fix.

2. Your phone model and OS version

Camera behaviour, image sizes and the on-device detection models differ enough between handsets that "it does not work" is unanswerable without this.

3. What the detection actually did

This is the important one, and it is the part people usually skip.

When a photo step fails or gives a result you did not expect, open the Detection details disclosure on that screen. It prints exactly what ran — how many of the four corner markers were seen, which decode sizes were tried, how sharp the frame was, and whether each detection engine loaded. Select that text and paste it into your email verbatim.

It matters because those cases look identical from the outside and are completely different problems. "No corner markers seen" is a framing or lighting problem you can fix in ten seconds. "Marker engine: FAILED TO LOAD" is my bug, on my side, and no amount of retaking the photo will help — that exact failure once masqueraded as a photo problem for weeks. The disclosure is there so you never have to guess which one you are looking at, and so I never have to guess either.

4. The photos

For anything involving the camera, the target, hole detection, scale or markers, the photo is the bug report. Please attach:

  • The original photo file from your gallery — not a screenshot of it, and not a crop.
  • If your mail app offers to resize images when attaching, choose Actual Size / Original. The compression on "Small" or "Medium" destroys precisely the fine detail the detector works on, so a shrunk photo cannot reproduce a detection bug.
  • If you used the before/after flow, send both the blank sheet and the shot sheet.
  • A screenshot of the results screen is a useful extra, but it is not a substitute for the original photo.

On your photos and your privacy. The app never uploads anything — if I have a photo of yours, it is because you attached it to an email yourself. I use it to reproduce your problem and nothing else. If you would rather not send a photo of your actual target, a photo of a printed sheet with a few holes punched in it reproduces most detection bugs just as well. Please do not send anything you would not want in someone else's inbox.

Before you write: three things that fix most problems

  1. Print at 100%. If you are using the free printable targets, the print dialog must be set to 100% / Actual size, never "Fit to page" or "Shrink to fit". Every sheet has a 1-inch and a 50 mm ruler printed on it — measure one before you shoot. A page scaled to 94% makes every measurement wrong by 6% and nothing on screen will tell you.
  2. Back up, do not zoom in. If the app cannot find the corner markers, the usual cause is framing too tight. All four corners need to be in the frame with a little white margin around them. Filling the frame with the bull strips the markers' quiet zone and you get zero markers found.
  3. Hold the phone square-on. The corner markers correct for a moderate angle, but an extreme oblique view will be rejected rather than silently guessed at.

Things I cannot do

Issue an App Store refund
Apple handles every iPhone refund itself; developers have no button for it. Use reportaproblem.apple.com. Details on the FAQ.
See your data
Your rifles, loads, sessions and photos live only on your phone. There is no account and no server, so I cannot look up your setup, recover a deleted session, or restore anything for you. Settings → Export all sessions (CSV) is your backup.
Tell you what your zero is
The app is advisory. It measures what you photographed and computes a correction from the specs you selected. It never certifies a zero — dial, then confirm with a follow-up group of live fire.

Feature requests

Send them. The single most useful message I have ever received was a tester telling me one sentence of onboarding copy was confusing; that turned into a rewrite of every instruction in the app. Small, specific complaints are worth more than long wish-lists — tell me the moment you got stuck.

For education and lawful sporting use. No app — this one included — certifies a zero. Confirm every zero and every dope change with live fire before relying on it, and follow all firearm safety rules and your range's procedures.
